\section{Taxonomy: what is being studied?}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Before any measurement, the object has to be named. Most disagreements about
whether a learning system ``has a phase transition'' are disagreements about
what was held fixed, and they are invisible until the coordinates are written
down. A registered atlas point is the seven-tuple
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A=(D,X,\delta,\Omega,\mathbf N,m,H),
  \label{eq:atlas-tuple}
\end{equation}
where $D$ is the system, $X$ the state object, $\delta$ the deformation,
$\Omega$ the disorder ensemble, $\mathbf N$ the scale coordinate and path, $m$
the observable, and $H$ the hypothesised phenomenon. Two studies can be
compared only after the components asserted to be equivalent have been named.

\subsection{The seven coordinates}

\paragraph{1. System $D$.}
The update law and interaction structure. This release spans perceptrons and
linear rules, soft committee machines, deep MLPs, single- and two-layer causal
attention models, a mixture-of-experts layer, and a contrastive encoder. A
family name is a root node, not a claim that its descendants share an ensemble.

\paragraph{2. State object $X$.}
Trained parameters, hidden representations, attention maps, the interpolating
path between two solutions, embedding covariance spectra, and epoch-resolved
trajectories all appear here. A single run may expose several; every observable
names the one it is defined on. This is why the primary observables of
\S\ref{sec:observables} live in function space --- it is the only state object
the systems above share.

\paragraph{3. Deformation $\delta$.}
What is varied: sample ratio, weight decay, initialisation scale, effective
temperature, InfoNCE temperature, task similarity, real-data fraction,
load-balancing weight, adapter rank, task-pool size, cue reliability, context
length. Width is a scale coordinate; replacing an activation is an
architectural deformation. Conflating the two makes a scaling statement
ambiguous.

\paragraph{4. Disorder ensemble $\Omega$.}
Initialisation, data draw, minibatch order, and evaluation sample are distinct
random variables. One draw of the \emph{outer} ensemble fixes an initialisation
\emph{and} a data set, and that is the unit of independence throughout
(\S\ref{sec:gates}). Declaring it is not bookkeeping: the susceptibility in
\eqref{eq:chi-binder} means nothing until it is fixed.

\paragraph{5. Scale path $\mathbf N$.}
Scale is a vector $(d,\text{width},\text{depth},n,L,\ldots)$. A finite-size
study declares one coordinate as $N$ and registers how the others move.
``A larger model'' is not a scaling path --- and, as \S\ref{sec:gates} makes
operational, neither is every second axis a scale path at all: a planted rank
or a generation count changes the problem rather than enlarging it.

\paragraph{6. Observable $m$.}
An estimator together with its units, normalisation, ensemble, null, and
validity range. Normalised risk, function-space overlap, replica overlap,
susceptibility, Binder cumulant, effective rank, kernel alignment, barrier
height, attention entropy and a causal ablation difference are different
observables even though all are dimensionless.

\paragraph{7. Phenomenon $H$.}
The hypothesis: recovery, specialisation, symmetry breaking, crossover,
criticality, multiplicity, dynamical delay, mechanism switching, or collapse.
A phenomenon is never assigned from the visual slope of a curve; it is
adjudicated by the registered diagnostics of \S\ref{sec:gates}.

\subsection{What is deliberately outside the taxonomy}

Theory status (exact, asymptotic, partial, none), fidelity (solvable,
trainable synthetic, natural, deployed) and evidence support (semantic,
replication, finite-size, predictive, interventional, external) are
\emph{orthogonal} metadata, not coordinates. Keeping them separate is what
prevents an exact solvable result from being called externally valid, and a
large natural-data experiment from being called exact theory. The protocol
fields --- question, estimator, null, holdout, intervention and falsifier ---
are separate for the same reason: the same atlas point can be tested well or
badly, and the tuple \eqref{eq:atlas-tuple} does not record which.

The outcome vocabulary is likewise not part of the taxonomy. It belongs to the
decision procedure and is stated once, in Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}.
