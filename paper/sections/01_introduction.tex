\section{Introduction}

Statistical mechanics offers machine learning more than analogies to phase
transitions: it offers explicit ensembles, scale coordinates, observables,
interventions, and falsifiable statements about collective behavior. Yet a
phase diagram drawn after the fact can merely summarize a simulator, and the
same word ``entropy'' can denote token uncertainty, macrostate multiplicity, or
trajectory diversity. We therefore study one umbrella question:
\begin{quote}
How can numerical statistical mechanics describe, compare, and falsify
collective phenomena across modern learning systems without confusing theory,
realism, and evidence?
\end{quote}

Our answer has three components. First, a seven-layer atlas states which state
object, deformation, disorder ensemble, scale path, observable, and phenomenon
are under study in each domain. Second, an experiment grammar maps these nouns
to executable specifications and typed estimates. Third, domain dossiers state
what was observed, what it may mean, and what remains unestablished.
\emph{Predictive phase continuation} is a particularly demanding protocol
inside this grammar: calibrate at an anchor, predict an untouched deformation,
diagnose breakdown, and test a frozen repair on independent evidence.

The domain coverage is deliberately asymmetric. Transformers receive the
deepest mechanism study; diffusion, reinforcement learning (RL), and
multi-agent systems test portability of the grammar. We do not propose one
universality class for these systems. The atlas instead separates a genuine
crossing from a censored threshold, an $O(1)$ risk from an extensive resource,
temporal fluctuations from disorder fluctuations, and a five-seed screen from
a finite-size phase claim.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an orthogonal seven-layer taxonomy, observable dictionary, 13-outcome
  vocabulary, and PhaseCard v3 that preserve domain-specific physics;
  \item a common execution and evidence grammar with typed estimates, nested
  disorder, explicit censoring, interventions, provenance, and invalidation;
  \item a held-out predictive-continuation protocol with nontrivial baselines
  and prospective largest-size evaluation;
  \item a Transformer flagship study that distinguishes broad exploratory
  coverage from frozen confirmation of module, optimizer, scaling, and data
  hypotheses; and
  \item diffusion, RL, and multi-agent dossiers, plus literature, question,
  implementation, claim, and artifact ledgers that distinguish unknown,
  unimplemented, run, invalidated, and supported cells.
\end{enumerate}
