\section{Learning paradigms beyond supervised regression}
\label{sec:paradigm}

The settings in this block are the furthest from analytic reach: self-supervised
representation learning, recursive training on generated data, sequential task
acquisition, conditional computation, and low-rank adaptation. Each is reduced
to a controlled teacher--student form in which a single deformation can be
dialled continuously, so that a boundary --- if one exists --- has somewhere to
be.

\subsection{Dimensional collapse in contrastive learning}

An encoder is trained with an InfoNCE objective on paired views of a planted
low-dimensional latent, and the order parameter is the normalised effective
rank $r_{\rm eff}$ of \eqref{eq:effrank}.

\paragraph{Observation.} Across the temperature range
$\AtlasContrastiveCollapseControlRange{}$ the normalised effective rank moves
over a range of $\AtlasContrastiveCollapseSemanticRatio{}$ times its own
$95\%$ interval, with the representation occupying a small fraction of the
available embedding directions at low temperature and expanding as temperature
rises. The family is graded
\AtlasContrastiveCollapseGrade{} with verdict
\emph{\AtlasContrastiveCollapseVerdict{}} over
\AtlasContrastiveCollapseConditions{} conditions.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Temperature controls how strongly the objective
penalises the uniformity term, and the effective rank responds to it
monotonically and far outside seed noise. Dimensional collapse in this setting
is a continuously tunable property of the objective, not an accident of
optimisation.

\paragraph{Not established.} We do not claim a critical temperature. Only one
finite-size signature is admissible, so the outcome is a candidate boundary; a
collapse that is sharp in the embedding dimension has not been demonstrated.

\paragraph{Next falsifier.} Extend the embedding-dimension path and test
whether the transition in $r_{\rm eff}$ sharpens with it. If the curve simply
translates without steepening, the effect is a crossover and the candidate
boundary should be withdrawn.

\subsection{Model collapse under recursive training}

Each generation trains on a mixture of a fraction $p_{\rm real}$ of fresh
teacher data and $1-p_{\rm real}$ of the previous generation's own output.

\paragraph{Observation.} The terminal teacher overlap falls monotonically as
$p_{\rm real}\to0$, moving over
$\AtlasModelCollapseSemanticRatio{}$ times its interval across
\AtlasModelCollapseConditions{} conditions; verdict
\emph{\AtlasModelCollapseVerdict{}}, grade \AtlasModelCollapseGrade{}.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} A finite real-data fraction anchors the loop. The
measured quantity is the anchoring \emph{strength} as a function of
$p_{\rm real}$, which is the input a theory of generational drift would need to
reproduce.

\paragraph{Not established.} Whether the anchoring is a true threshold in
$p_{\rm real}$ or a smooth degradation is not resolved. The second axis is the
generation count, which advances the process rather than enlarging one system,
so it is not a scale path and no finite-size claim is available --- the verdict
is \emph{\AtlasModelCollapseVerdict{}}.

\paragraph{Next falsifier.} Repeat the sweep with model width as the second
axis at a fixed generation count. If the degradation curve steepens with width,
the anchoring becomes a candidate transition; if it merely translates, model
collapse is a smooth erosion and should never be described as a phase.

\subsection{Forgetting and transfer under task similarity}

Two teachers are constructed with an exact correlation dial
$\rho$, training proceeds on task~A and then task~B, and retention of A is
measured against a from-scratch baseline on B.

\paragraph{Observation.} Forgetting spans
$\AtlasContinualForgettingSemanticRatio{}$ times its interval over
$\rho\in\AtlasContinualForgettingControlRange{}$, and the susceptibility peak
height grows with the scale path as $N^{\AtlasContinualForgettingSharpening{}}$.
The verdict is \emph{\AtlasContinualForgettingVerdict{}}.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The response is genuinely critical --- the disorder
susceptibility diverges along the sampled scale path --- but the location of
the boundary does not extrapolate within the sampled window, so the interference
threshold in $\rho$ is measured to exist and not measured to be anywhere in
particular.

\paragraph{Not established.} No value of $\rho$ is reported as a critical task
similarity. The transfer sign change is visible but is not certified by an
admissible location fit.

\paragraph{Next falsifier.} Widen the $\rho$ grid past the current window and
re-fit \eqref{eq:drift}; if $c_\infty$ becomes admissible and interior, the
verdict upgrades, and if the peaks continue to march outward the correct
conclusion is that similarity is not the controlling variable.

\subsection{Expert specialisation and load balancing}

A mixture-of-experts layer is trained on a target composed of distinct
sub-functions, with the load-balancing coefficient as the control.

\paragraph{Observation.} The specialisation gap \eqref{eq:specgap} moves by only
$\AtlasMoeSpecializationSemanticRatio{}$ times its $95\%$ interval across the
sampled range. The verdict is \emph{\AtlasMoeSpecializationVerdict{}}.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} This is a negative result and is reported as one.
Under the registered budget the expert assignment does not separate from its
uncertainty, so no claim about symmetry breaking among experts is supported ---
in either direction.

\paragraph{Not established.} We cannot conclude that load balancing has no
effect; we conclude that this design lacks the resolution to detect one.

\paragraph{Next falsifier.} Increase the contrast between sub-functions and the
number of experts. If the gap remains within noise at higher contrast, the
absence becomes a substantive finding rather than a resolution limit.

\subsection{A rank threshold for low-rank adaptation}

A frozen backbone is adapted by a rank-$r$ update to a target displaced by a
planted perturbation of known rank $r^\ast$.

\paragraph{Observation.} Recovery of the planted subspace, measured by
\eqref{eq:subspace}, rises from near chance to essentially complete across the
adapter-rank grid, spanning
$\AtlasLoraRankSemanticRatio{}$ times its interval over
\AtlasLoraRankConditions{} conditions; verdict \emph{\AtlasLoraRankVerdict{}},
grade \AtlasLoraRankGrade{}.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Adapter capacity behaves like a storage constraint:
below the planted rank the update cannot represent the required displacement,
and above it recovery saturates. The threshold is not merely near $r^\ast$ but
\emph{locked} to it --- for every planted rank in the sweep, the disorder
susceptibility has its maximum exactly at $r=r^\ast$, and recovery saturates
from that point on. This is the cleanest capacity statement in the block and
the closest of these settings to something a Gardner-style calculation could
plausibly address.

\paragraph{Not established.} Nothing thermodynamic. The second axis here is the
planted rank $r^\ast$, which changes the problem rather than the size of one
system, so it is not a scale path and the finite-size machinery of
\S\ref{sec:gates} does not apply --- hence the verdict
\emph{\AtlasLoraRankVerdict{}}. The threshold is located along the control
axis; whether it \emph{sharpens} is a separate question this design cannot
answer.

\paragraph{Next falsifier.} Hold $r^\ast$ fixed and sweep the backbone width as
a genuine scale path. A capacity transition requires the step in $r$ to sharpen
with width; if the transition width stays constant, the effect is an
approximation-error crossover rather than a transition, and the present result
remains a locked threshold with no critical content.
