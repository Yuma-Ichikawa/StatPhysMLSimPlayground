\begin{abstract}
The statistical mechanics of learning has exact results for a thin slice of
settings --- shallow architectures, Gaussian inputs, the proportional
high-dimensional limit --- and modern machine learning lives almost entirely
outside it. Rather than describe that outside with borrowed vocabulary, we
measure it. We present a numerical atlas of \AtlasTotalFamilies{} learning
settings across \AtlasTotalBlocks{} blocks, spanning symmetry breaking,
training dynamics and loss-landscape geometry, and learning paradigms beyond
supervised regression. Every setting is
measured with the same function-space order parameters, on a control grid fine
enough to place a boundary, along a scale path long enough to extrapolate, over
an ensemble of independent disorder seeds large enough to make fluctuations
mean something: \AtlasTotalRuns{} trained models over
\AtlasTotalConditions{} registered conditions.

The atlas is anchored rather than free-floating. \AtlasAnchorFamilies{}
families reproduce settings with closed-form theory and report the measured
quantity against that theory with the residual as a first-class number;
\AtlasNoTheoryFamilies{} families have no theory at all. A decision procedure
converts curves into named outcomes and is designed to refuse: it separates a
diverging response from a located boundary, requires independent finite-size
signatures to agree before they accumulate, and censors a maximum sitting at
the edge of the sampled grid rather than calling it a critical point.

The procedure's calibration is the headline result. Exactly one of
\AtlasTotalFamilies{} families received the verdict \emph{transition
supported}, and it is the Gardner storage capacity --- the anchor whose answer
was known in advance, recovered at
$\alpha=\AtlasPerceptronCapacityPeakControl{}$ against the exact $\alpha_c=2$,
with a peak height diverging as
$N^{\AtlasPerceptronCapacitySharpening{}}$. On the fourteen settings whose
answers are not known, the same gates certified nothing. Applied to our own
flagship family they initially returned a transition claim that proved to be an
artefact of a coarse control grid and a fixed epoch budget --- under which
wider models were simply optimised less --- and the corrected design is what
the paper reports.

The outcome is a map with quantitative, falsifiable entries: measured
sharpening exponents, boundary locations with intervals, scaling paths on which
finite-size peaks align, and explicit negative and unresolved cells. Each is
stated together with the experiment that would overturn it, so that the entries
function as targets for the theory that does not yet exist rather than as
illustrations of a theory that does.
\end{abstract}
