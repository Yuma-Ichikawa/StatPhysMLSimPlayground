Figure~\ref{fig:atlas-map} is the release in one view. Of
\AtlasTotalFamilies{} families, the procedure returned a spread rather than a
uniform verdict, and the spread is the point: a method that returns
``supported'' everywhere has not been tested.

\subsection{The calibration result}

The single most informative entry in the map is which family passed. Exactly
\AtlasCountPhaseTransitionSupported{} of \AtlasTotalFamilies{} families
received the verdict \emph{transition supported}, and it is the Gardner
capacity --- the anchor whose answer was known before the experiment was run
(\S\ref{sec:anchors}). Applied to every other setting in the release --- none of whose
answers are known --- the same procedure certified nothing.

We take that as the central evidence that the gates are calibrated rather than
merely strict. A procedure that never fires is useless; one that fires
everywhere is worse. This one fired once, on the case placed there to make it
fire, and the boundary it reported,
$\alpha=\AtlasPerceptronCapacityPeakControl{}$, sits at the grid point nearest
the exact $\alpha_c=2$.

\subsection{Transferable findings}

\paragraph{1. At eight seeds, a quarter of susceptibility curves carry a
single-point artefact.}
Across the release, \AtlasSpikeRejected{} of \AtlasSpikeTotal{} size-curves have
their global susceptibility maximum at an isolated spike: one diverged run out
of eight inflates the variance at exactly one control value, and the neighbours
sit at the floor. A plain argmax reports each of these as a boundary, and its
height then enters the sharpening fit. Rejecting them --- by requiring a
neighbour to rise with the peak, which a genuine critical swelling does and an
outlier does not --- changed the verdict of several families in this paper,
including our own headline. We would not have found this without measuring the
same quantity at several sizes and asking whether the answers agreed.

The practical recommendation is uncomfortable but simple: at this replication,
a single susceptibility peak is not evidence. Either replicate to twelve or
more seeds, or report the peak together with an explicit test that it is not
one seed's excursion.

\paragraph{2. The control parameter is a hypothesis --- and testing it needs
more seeds than we had.}
The specialisation family's peak locations drift with size in $\alpha=n/d$,
which would mean the boundary lies on $n\propto d^{\gamma}$ rather than on the
proportional path. With six admissible sizes the fitted exponent excluded
$\gamma=1$; with the three that survive the spike test it is
$\gamma=\AtlasSpecializationGamma{}$ with interval
\AtlasSpecializationGammaCI{}, which does not. We report the weaker statement
(\S\ref{sec:symmetry}). The question is worth someone's twelve-seed budget: if
$\alpha$ is the wrong abscissa, a study sweeping it at a single size is not
holding the boundary fixed, and two such studies at different sizes can
disagree without either being wrong.

\paragraph{3. Diverging response and locatable boundary are different
achievements, and the second is much harder.}
Several families produce a susceptibility that grows convincingly with size and
still fail to yield a boundary location: the peak marches out of the sampled
window, or the drift extrapolation and the collapse name places that differ by
more than the consistency tolerance. Reporting only the first --- the usual
practice --- would have produced several confident transition claims here that
the location analysis does not support. We suggest that a susceptibility plot
without an accompanying drift fit should be read as evidence of criticality and
not as evidence of a critical point.

\paragraph{4. Two design choices can manufacture or destroy a transition.}
Both were found the hard way in this work. A control grid too coarse to hold an
interior maximum yields a structurally censored result regardless of the
physics; and a fixed epoch budget makes wider models appear \emph{less} ordered,
because they are less optimised, which inverts the sign of the finite-size
trend. Any finite-size claim about a learning system should therefore report
grid density and the optimisation stopping rule alongside the seed count. We
recommend training to a convergence criterion, and recording per run whether it
was met, so that optimisation shortfall is a measured quantity rather than a
hidden covariate.

\paragraph{5. A risk curve can be flat while the mechanism reverses.}
In the positional--semantic family (\S\ref{sec:sequence}) the model's risk
barely moves across most of the control range, while the causal ablation shows
the learned solution swinging from purely positional to purely semantic. Had we
measured only performance, we would have reported nothing happening. This is a
general hazard for claims about attention: the behavioural quantity that is
easiest to plot is often the one least sensitive to the mechanism the claim is
about, and an intervention that removes one route at a time costs little more
than the evaluation itself.

The same experiment illustrates the value of registering a predicted location.
Giving both cues a finite reliability $1-p$ and $1-q$ makes a
reliability-following model switch near $p=q$; the measured sign change lands
close to it, which is a far stronger statement than observing that a switch
occurred somewhere. When the semantic cue is left perfect --- the usual setup ---
it wins for every $p>0$ and the apparent transition is a property of the data,
not of the model.

\subsection{What we would do next, in order}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Twelve seeds, everywhere.} Replication is the binding
    constraint on every family here, and it is also what makes the spike test
    reject a quarter of the size-curves. Twelve seeds would lift the replication
    tier and shrink the artefact rate at the same time; nothing else changes.
  \item \textbf{Re-run the drifting families on the rescaled control
    $n/d^{\gamma}$.} If the peaks return to the interior of the window, several
    unresolved boundaries become locatable in a single sweep.
  \item \textbf{A frozen prospective replication.} No result here has been
    confirmed at fresh seeds after the analysis was fixed, which is the one
    gate that no amount of re-analysis can substitute for.
  \item \textbf{One natural-data endpoint.} External validity is zero across
    the release by construction. A single family carried through to a natural
    corpus would establish whether any of these boundaries survive contact with
    real data, which is the question the whole programme is ultimately for.
\end{enumerate}
