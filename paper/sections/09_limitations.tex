\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Two evidence components are zero everywhere.}
Replication is no longer the binding constraint: every condition is run at
\AtlasSpecializationSeeds{} outer seeds, which is the tier the grading scheme
requires. What caps the release now is that \emph{intervention} and
\emph{external validity} score zero for every family. No family is evaluated on
natural data or a pretrained model, and only the attention block performs a
causal ablation --- which is scored under its own order parameter rather than as
an intervention on a boundary claim. Grade~A consequently requires a family that
is both replicated and theory-anchored, and exactly one is.

\paragraph{No prospective confirmation.}
Nothing here has been re-run frozen at fresh seeds after the analysis was
fixed. Every number is therefore a retrospective estimate on the data that
also informed the design choices, and the sharpening exponents in particular
should be read as effective values over the sampled window rather than as
critical exponents.

\paragraph{No natural data and no pretrained models.}
Every input distribution is synthetic and every teacher is planted. This is
what makes the order parameters exactly interpretable, and it is also the
strongest limit on external validity: the external-validity component of the
evidence vector is zero for every family in the release. Whether any boundary
located here survives contact with natural data is untested.

\paragraph{Small models.}
Widths are in the tens to low hundreds and models have thousands of parameters.
The design argument for this is given in \S\ref{sec:intro} --- fluctuation
scaling needs many independent runs more than it needs any large one --- but it
means the asymptotic regime may not have been entered in every family, and a
drift fit that is inadmissible at these sizes may become admissible at larger
ones.

\paragraph{The spike test trades false boundaries for missed ones.}
Requiring a susceptibility maximum to have a shoulder (\S\ref{sec:gates})
rejects single-run excursions, but it also rejects a peak that is genuinely
narrow because the control grid is coarse relative to its width. The
positional--semantic family is the clearest case: a maximum near $p\approx0.13$
is present at all four widths, which is not how an artefact behaves, and it is
nonetheless rejected at three of them. We keep the conservative rule because
this program is more damaged by inventing boundaries than by missing them, but
a rejected peak here should be read as a reason to refine the grid or add
seeds, not as evidence that nothing is there.

\paragraph{The Binder cumulant is a poor diagnostic for one-sided order
parameters.} Several families use an order parameter that is non-negative by
construction, such as the specialisation gap or a subspace overlap. The
cumulant in \eqref{eq:chi-binder} is designed for a symmetric distribution
around zero, and for these quantities its crossings are hard to interpret and
mostly failed the consistency test. Where a crossing is reported as absent, the
reader should attribute part of that to the diagnostic and not only to the
system.

\paragraph{Optimiser and architecture are held fixed within a family.}
Each family sweeps one control and one scale coordinate; the optimiser, the
activation, and the initialisation scheme are fixed. A boundary located here is
a boundary of that configuration, and the atlas does not establish that it is a
property of the task alone.
