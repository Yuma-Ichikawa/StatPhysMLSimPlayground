\section{Conclusion}

The atlas turns statistical-mechanics language into an auditable numerical
practice. Its seven layers state what is studied; the PhaseCard states the
ensemble, scale path, observable, alternative, intervention, and holdout; and
the evidence vector states what may be claimed. Predictive phase continuation
then turns selected atlas edges into prospective transport tests. Failure is
informative: a boundary can shift, round, become censored, lose semantic
meaning, or expose model misspecification without being forced into one
universal story.

The Transformer study provides the deepest module, optimizer, scaling, and data
tests. Diffusion, RL, and multi-agent systems show how the same evidence
grammar can be used for trajectory, feedback, and population dynamics while
retaining domain-specific observables. The central deliverable is therefore
not twenty unrelated plots or a claim that modern ML shares one thermodynamic
limit. It is an extensible map from questions to implementations, estimates,
outcomes, claims, invalidations, and next falsifiers, with every untested cell
remaining visible.
